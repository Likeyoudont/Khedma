# Fee recommendation for the launch

The current implementation remains **10% from the client and 10% from the worker**, as previously chosen. The later question about 5% was a request for an opinion, so it has not changed that rate.

My launch recommendation is to test **5% each** as an introductory rate. This is a pricing experiment, not proof that the business will be profitable. For a 200 DH job:

| Rate from each participant | Client’s total | Worker’s earnings after fee | Khedma’s gross fees |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10% | 220 DH | 180 DH | 40 DH |
| 5% | 210 DH | 190 DH | 20 DH |

Khedma’s gross fees still need to cover payment processing, support, refunds and customer acquisition. Two separate fee payments may each carry processing costs. Cash Plus does not publish one universal merchant rate on its [business payment page](https://www.cashplus.ma/services-professionnels/cash-plus-payment); obtain the merchant quote before deciding whether 5% is sustainable. No provider price is assumed here.

There are three money amounts in a fee-enabled agreement: the job price paid to the worker, the client’s fee paid to Khedma, and the worker’s fee paid to Khedma. A third-party payment provider is the company that processes the online fee payments and settles them to Khedma’s merchant account. Its processing charge is a business cost, not an extra fee silently added to the client’s quote.
